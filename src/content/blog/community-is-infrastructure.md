When we talk about infrastructure, we usually mean the technical layer beneath a product. Communities have an infrastructure layer too: the habits, pathways, and shared context that make participation possible.

## Design the first contribution

“Everyone is welcome” is a sentiment. A clear first issue, a friendly review, and an obvious place to ask questions form a system. New contributors need a path they can see and a first step small enough to take.

The goal is not simply to gather people in a room. It is to help someone move from curiosity to contribution, and from contribution to ownership.

## Documentation is hospitality

Good documentation says: we expected you. It removes the private knowledge that quietly separates insiders from everyone else. This applies to codebases, event teams, and community programs in exactly the same way.

## Maintain the maintainers

Healthy communities distribute responsibility. If every decision and activity depends on one energetic person, growth only makes the system more fragile. Create roles, rotate them, record decisions, and make rest part of the operating model.

The best community infrastructure becomes almost invisible. People feel supported, useful work moves forward, and new leaders have room to emerge.
